# OFFCUT — the part that needs real paper

You do not need to understand the code. We need to check whether the instructions work with paper you can hold.

## Do this first

1. Find one **A4 sheet: 21 × 29.7 cm**. Put it upright on a flat table, with the short edge at the top. US Letter paper is a different size; do not label it A4.
2. Place **4–6 flat, opaque coloured scraps** on it. Use colours that stand out against the reference sheet. Leave a gap between every scrap; keep them inside the sheet and avoid curled or overlapping paper.
3. Take a photo from directly above in even light. Include **all four A4 corners**. Hold the phone still so the edges are sharp.
4. Attach that photograph in this chat. Keep the physical scraps in the same arrangement until you have saved the cutting plan. This is the first real test; one photo is enough to get started.

If you have no printer yet, you can still do the photo and software test. Physical print calibration and assembly will need a printer and ruler. Do not replace real photographs or assembly evidence with AI-made images.

## Then run the real workflow

Open https://offcut-six.vercel.app/ and upload your photo, rather than “Try illustrated example.” Confirm the A4 corners. Let detection finish. Check that each mask covers only paper, including torn edges and holes. Use the correction tools if needed, and confirm review only when you have actually checked it.

Choose a **6 × 6** Heart, Flower or Bird. If the app says there is not enough material, add paper or correct an erroneous mask; do not force the review or pretend missing pieces exist. Click one artwork square to find its source. Exclude a scrap and check that the app produces a valid new collage or an honest shortage. Restore it before saving the plan you intend to cut.

## Make one small result

You need the saved real scraps, a printer, a millimetre ruler, scissors, glue and backing paper.

1. Download the cutting and assembly plans from the real-photo project. Open the downloaded HTML file.
2. Print the calibration page at **100% / actual size**, with **fit-to-page disabled**. Measure both sides of its square: each must be **20 mm**. If either is wrong, correct print scaling and repeat before cutting.
3. Print the remaining plans with exactly the same settings. The source maps show where to cut on your original paper; you cut the real scraps, not the printout. Align each location using the A4 reference and locator/offset labels.
4. Cut each numbered **12 mm** source square once from its primary cutting page. Keep its number beside it or on the back. Overlapping source-map viewports are context, not permission to cut the same source twice.
5. Arrange the numbered pieces in the assembly grid, left to right and top to bottom, then glue them to the backing. A 6 × 6 gapless result is **72 × 72 mm**.
6. Photograph the original materials, the app preview, the numbered plans, the ruler/calibration square and the assembled result. Keep the photos and note any confusion or mismatch. If something fails, record the failure instead of calling it a successful assembly.

Attach the original input photo and the proof photos in this chat. They let the submission show what was actually made. If there is no time for all ten inputs, submit the strongest honest software entry on time and state the remaining evidence gap explicitly.
